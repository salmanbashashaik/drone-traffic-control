**The probe harness.** `<b1review>/sim.py` copies the fuzz dynamics:
- a double integrator with a 100 ms zero-order hold;

For every step it records:
- the minimum separation;
- fallbacks, solver errors and exceptions;
- latch flips, meaning a pair that stays latched while its axis changes;
- "contradictory" steps.

A step is contradictory when both of these hold:
- a drone is squeezed between two latched neighbours (the cosine between the directions to them is below -0.7);
- the two latches push it in roughly opposite world directions (the dot product of the sideways directions is below -0.5).

The sideways direction is computed exactly as in `tests/test_filter_latch.py`: `_frame_lateral(n, latch.axis)`, negated for the second drone of the pair.

## Verdict

**Not ready.**

No safety-metric failure was found. No probe in this review had any of the following:
- a separation below d_eff = 5.5 m;
- a fallback;
- a solver error;
- an exception;
- a latch flip.

The B1 requirement is that a drone squeezed between two neighbours gets opposite latches towards them. That requirement holds only in the collinear geometry that the new tests cover.

The requirement fails in a "bent" sandwich: the two neighbours are 10° to 45° off a straight line and arrive staged. There, the new code gives the squeezed drone contradictory latches at every bearing tried. It also does this more often than `489a2a0` did. The cause is one ordering choice in the new seeded colouring (HIGH-1).

A second defect predates this PR. It makes the colouring itself wrong whenever the drone's two lines of sight straddle the world x = ±y diagonal (MEDIUM-1).

The founder should decide whether B1 must hold for these geometries. If it must, the B1 claim in DECISIONS.md and INTERFACES.md is not yet true.

## Findings

### HIGH-1: a far outer pair latched from a 3 s prediction closes the odd cycle

In bent staged sandwiches, the squeezed drone gets contradictory latches. This happens more often than in 489a2a0.

**Location in `src/utm_services/safety/filter.py`:**
- `:432-433`: conflict entry uses `min(|r|, |r + v t*|)`, with `t*` clamped to 3 s at `:300`.
- `:451-458`: every latched pair of the cluster becomes a rank-2 or rank-1 seed.
- `:459-463`: the colouring call.
- `:472-479`: a new pair with colour sign 0, in a cluster of 3 or more, gets origin `"none"`.
- `:538`: seeded edges are coloured first, highest rank first, before nearer unseeded edges.

**Geometry.**
- Drone b hovers.
- Drone a arrives from 30 m at 10 m/s along bearing θ.
- Drone c arrives from `dc` m at 10 m/s along bearing θ + 180° − bend.
- With bend = 0 this is exactly the staged sandwich in `tests/test_filter_latch.py`.

**Mechanism.** `<b1review>/p1h_mech.py` shows it for θ = 0, bend = 10°, dc = 50 m, on the new tree:

```

```


What happens:
1. The outer pair a-c enters conflict while it is still 69.76 m apart. Its predicted point of closest approach at t* = 3 s is within 12 m.
2. Its predicted miss of 1.49 m counts as clear (> 0.5 m), so a-c latches as `fixed`.
3. The inner pair b-c enters at 41 m. By then a-b and a-c are both rank-2 seeds, so both are coloured before b-c. This fixes a and c relative to each other.
4. b-c then closes an odd cycle with equal colours. It gets sign 0 and latches `"none"`, which means the turn-right frame.
5. The squeezed drone b ends up with sideways directions [0, −1, 0] and [0.174, 0.985, 0] from its two latches. These point in opposite directions.

In the straight case (bend 0), b-c is nearer than a-c when b-c enters. So b-c is coloured first and the result is consistent. That is why the new test passes.

**Impact.**
- Separation stayed above d_eff in every run.
- The minimum separation moved in both directions between versions: 6.062 m against 5.928 m at bend 10°, and 5.993 m against 6.014 m at bend 45°.
- The defect is a broken design property, not a measured collision. The squeezed drone is told to move opposite ways by its two neighbours. That is the exact B1 failure the change was meant to remove.
- In the bent staged geometry, the change makes that failure more common, not less.
- No test covers bent or diagonal geometry. All five tests in `tests/test_filter_latch.py` are collinear or axis-aligned.

**Possible directions for the author (not verified):**
- Do not seed from a pair that was latched only from a far prediction.
- Or order seeded edges by distance within a rank.
- Or let an inner pair that closes an odd cycle win over the outer pair, because the outer pair's drones are not squeezed.

### MEDIUM-1: the escape axes flip across the x = ±y diagonal

The sign-normalised escape axes flip sign across the x = ±y diagonal. So the colouring is meaningless for a drone whose two lines of sight straddle it. This predates the PR, but one exact-45° case got worse.

**Location.** `src/utm_services/safety/filter.py:500-509` (`_escape_axes`). The normalisation is `axis if axis[argmax|axis|] > 0 else -axis`.

The docstring at `:490-492` says this "gives pairs on opposite sides of a drone the same axes". That is false when one line of sight has |x| > |y| and the other has |y| > |x|. The colouring code from `:538` assumes the two pairs share the axis. When they do not, colour +1 on both pairs means opposite world directions.

### LOW-1: INTERFACES.md describes the mirror trigger more narrowly than the code

`INTERFACES.md:86` says: "A turn-right latch that contradicts that colouring is re-oriented once".

The code at `src/utm_services/safety/filter.py:459-468` mirrors the latch when `escape[pair]` disagrees with the seed. `escape` comes from `_escape_signs`, which uses a clear predicted miss first and the colouring only when there is none (docstring at `:520-531`). So a clear miss alone can mirror a turn-right latch. DECISIONS.md describes this correctly.

Evidence: code reading (`sed -n 440,482p` and `sed -n 520,535p` on filter.py).

### LOW-2: seeds on edges that join an existing component are silently ignored

Location: `src/utm_services/safety/filter.py:588-598` (`_two_colour`).
- The seed value `first` is used only when both drones are uncoloured.
- When a seeded edge reaches a drone that is already coloured, propagation overwrites the latched colour.
- Nothing is logged or flagged.

So two contradictory fixed seeds in one component are never detected. This is part of the HIGH-1 mechanism: the a-c seed fixes c only through a's colour.

Evidence: code reading (`sed -n 588,602p`).

## What held

- **Fast tests pass.** `python -m pytest tests/test_filter_latch.py tests/test_filter_stub.py -q` gave `27 passed in 3.08s`.
- **Every caller passes a latch, and the runner keeps one per episode.**
  - `grep -n "filter_latch\|apply_filter(" src/utm_services/simulation/runner.py` shows `101: filter_latch: dict = {}` inside `run_episode`, and `135: ... apply_filter(observed, proposed, filter_config, latch=filter_latch)`.
  - `grep -rn "apply_filter(" src tests scripts --include=*.py | grep -v latch | grep -v "def apply_filter"` printed nothing (exit 1).
  - The reviewer read the multi-line calls in all the tests and verify scripts in the PR. Each one passes `latch=`.
  - The disturbance spy in `tests/test_disturbances.py:186-192` forwards `**kwargs`.
- **A non-dict latch raises.** `filter.py:1218-1219` raises `TypeError` with a UTM-077 message. Only the container type is checked, not its contents. A dict holding junk values was not probed.
- **Input-order invariance.** The reviewer ran a 5-drone converging ring (an odd cycle) and shuffled the order of states and actions on every step. The trajectories were bitwise identical: `ring5 trajectories bitwise equal under shuffle: True max abs diff: 0.0`. The run had min_sep 5.665 m, with 0 fallbacks, 0 solver errors, 0 flips and 0 contradictory steps (`p2_misc.py`).
- **No stale latch across episodes, in the probed case.** One dict was reused across two 2-drone episodes. It was empty after each one (`latch after: {}`), and min_sep was 6.889 m, the same as with a fresh dict.
- **Latch axes never changed while a pair stayed latched.** Every probe above reports `n_flips=0` / `flips 0` (P1b, P1c, P1d, P1e, P1g, P2).
- **No failures in about 500 closed-loop encounters (P1–P2).** None of them had a fallback, a solver error, an exception, or a separation below 5.5 m. This does not replace the fuzz numbers, which were not rerun.
- **The straight staged sandwich is fixed.**
  - Contradictory bearings fell from 16/60 to 1/60. The remaining one is the 45.0° tie in MEDIUM-1.
  - At θ = 0, min_sep rose from 5.657 m to 6.366 m.
- **Mirror algebra is correct.** The mirror at `filter.py:464-468` negates the axis and both signs. Since `_frame_lateral(n, −axis) = −_frame_lateral(n, axis)`, the sideways part reverses while `signs[1]·(n × lateral)` stays the same. So the vertical escape is kept, as the comment says. Checked by reading `:495-498`.
- **Exactly vertical stack.** Two drones 8 m apart on a vertical line latched `turn_right` with axis `[0.0, 1.0, 0.0]`. Nothing raised (`p2_misc.py`).
- **Floor arithmetic.** The test floors are 3.69, 3.01, 3.01, 3.15, 3.15, 2.61 and 2.69. Each is a measured sandwich value (mid 3.7402, floor 3.0630/3.0605, ceiling 3.2008, vertical 2.6680/2.7437) minus 0.05 m, rounded down to the centimetre. They match DECISIONS.md and P2-SA-02. Checked by reading the test files and the docs.
- **Documentation honesty.** P2-SA-02 §5.1 no longer claims that turn-right is the aviation convention under noise. The `filter.py` docstring no longer says "aviation head-on convention". Checked by reading the diff.
