Reviewer: fresh subagent on Fable 5.1 (author: Opus 5.5). Scope: per-cluster KKT-exact solves (DAQP / OSQP with a KKT certificate), the 595.6 m range gate, the threaded rollout, and the per-cluster solve deadlines with backup fallback (contract v1.5). Evidence read: `docs/results/deadline-2026-10-24.md`, `docs/results/scale-step3-2026-10-24.md`, `docs/results/solver-exact-2026-10-24.jsonl`, `tests/test_filter_deadline.py`, `INTERFACES.md`, `docs/DECISIONS.md`, `src/utm_services/safety/filter.py`, `scripts/verify/scale_bench.py`.

The full tier was not rerun (it ran on this code path). The reviewer ran the fast suite once and nine targeted probes, committed in `pr26-probes/` next to this file with a README. Machine: Windows 11, 12 logical cores, Python 3.11, osqp 0.6.7.post3, probes run with Numba's default thread count. This machine is about two times slower than the author's on the same scene (steady-state steps of `scale_bench` N = 64 groups of 8: 94-125 ms here against p50 48 / p95 62 ms in the PR). Absolute times below carry that caveat; the structural findings do not depend on it.

## Verdict: REQUEST CHANGES

Three blocking items, all on the deadline layer and its results document. The exactness work (attacks 1, 3, 5) and the switch wiring (attack 4) hold up; see "Verified correct".

## Blocking items

### B1. The "bounded worst step" claim excludes the first step, which costs 0.5-3 s at N = 64 (latch creation)

`docs/results/deadline-2026-10-24.md` says the single 64-drone cluster's worst step is 134 ms (152 ms on one thread) against 55 s without the deadline, and that at the target density "nothing changes". `scale_bench.py` measures steps 5 to 54 (`WARMUP, TIMED = 5, 50`, line 45; `if k >= WARMUP:` line 215). Step 0 of the same scenes, on this machine:

| scene (`dense_states(64, 0, cluster)`) | step 0 | of which `_update_latches` | missed agents at step 0 | steps 1-7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| groups of 64 | 3023 ms | 2803 ms | 64 | 216-295 ms |
| groups of 32 | 1442 ms | 1255 ms | 64 | 204-231 ms |
| groups of 8 (the target) | 534 ms | 441 ms | 6 | 94-125 ms |

Every episode of `run_episode` starts with an empty latch dict, so every N = 64 episode pays this on its first step, and at the target density six drones fly the unverified backup on that step. The deadline cannot bound it: the doc says so for part 1 in general, but the claim of a 134 ms worst step and of "nothing changes at the target" is made without the step that dominates. The cost is per entering pair in Python, about 2 ms per pair here (1.2-1.3 s for 530 entering pairs on a 64-ring in `probe2c_where.py`, 2.8 s for 1309 in the groups-of-64 scene), most of it `np.cross` and `np.linalg.norm` on single 3-vectors inside `_new_latch` and `_escape_axes` (cProfile, which inflates small-call times: 4770 `cross` calls take 42 % of `_update_latches`, `_escape_axes` 59 % cumulative). The PR touches `_update_latches` (vectorised `_seed_alongs`) and introduces the benchmark that drops these steps, so this is in scope.

Required: measure and report step 0 (or the full first second) of each scene in the results doc, and either scope the bound claim to steps after the latches exist or make latch creation cheap (vectorise `_new_latch` / `_escape_axes` over the entering pairs as `_seed_alongs` already does). The founder's acceptance of the "safety cost" was given against the post-warm-up numbers only.

Reproduce (from this directory):

    python probe2d_bench_first.py      # step 0-7 phase split of scale_bench's own scenes
    python probe2e_prof.py             # cProfile of _update_latches on a 64-drone first step

### B2. The per-cluster share guarantee does not hold: a far 3-drone cluster gets no solve because a 64-drone cluster overran by 0.7 ms

The doc (per-cluster bullet) and INTERFACES v1.5 promise that cluster k + 1 "still starts with at least r_(k+1)" and that "a slow cluster cannot delay another". Two mechanisms break it, both outside the test's model (`test_a_slow_cluster_does_not_delay_the_other` uses a cooperative fake solver that stops at its own deadline):

1. **Shares are smaller than solver overrun granularity in the 25 ms floor regime.** On a 64-ring at (300, 500) plus three drones at (800, 800) (500 m apart, no shared row), deadline on, step 0: the 64-cluster (367 variables, OSQP first) entered with 19.6 ms left and returned `_DEADLINE` after 20.3 ms (OSQP setup is outside its time limit, as the doc says). The 3-drone cluster then entered with -0.1 ms left and was marked missed at entry, 0 ms of solving. All 67 drones flew the backup; the three far drones because of a cluster they cannot interact with. Their accelerations differ from the deadline-off run. In the floor regime (`_MIN_SOLVE_WINDOW_S`, which is the regime of every N = 64 step on this machine and of N >= 128 on the author's), a 12-variable cluster behind a 367-variable one has a share of about 0.6 ms, below any solver's setup time.

2. **DAQP's time limit excludes its setup, like OSQP's, and the setup is large.** `_solve_daqp` at 2208 variables (the 64-ring with all rows) runs 213 ms at `time_limit=1 ms` and 231 ms at 5 ms; at 592 variables 13.9 / 13.5 ms. The doc states the opposite ("DAQP checks its time limit inside its iterations") and sizes the 25 ms floor on OSQP's 10 ms setup. DAQP is reached above 256 variables whenever OSQP returns a `solved` status that fails `_kkt_certified` (observed once on the 64-ring in the deadline-off baseline of `probe2b_starve.py`: "filter: _solve_osqp returned no KKT-certified solution"); `_solve_exact` then calls DAQP with the time left and the step runs 200 ms past every later cluster's deadline.

Required: a per-cluster minimum share (at least the measured setup time of the solver that will run, or solve the small clusters first when the window is at the floor), DAQP's setup overrun in the doc and in the floor derivation, and a test with the real solvers (two clusters, the first large) that asserts the second cluster is solved, not a fake that respects its deadline.

Reproduce:

    python probe2c_where.py            # per-cluster "left_at_entry" and the phase split; cluster b: -0.1 ms, DEADLINE
    python probe2b_starve.py           # the same scene end to end, cluster b missed, accelerations differ from deadline off
    python probe2_overrun.py           # DAQP 213 ms past a 1 ms limit at 2208 vars; OSQP setup 9.6 ms

### B3. The 10 ms post-solve reserve is exceeded by the backup actions on exactly the steps the deadline fires

The reserve `_POST_SOLVE_RESERVE_S` = 10 ms is sized from "at most 3.4 ms at N = 64 and 9.6 ms at N = 128" for part 3, which includes "backup actions for missed clusters". On a missed 64-cluster, `_evasive_accel` runs once per missed drone over all the cluster's QP pairs (`filter.py:2116`, loop at 1812), so part 3 is O(agents x pairs): 120-145 ms per step here (steps 1-7 of the groups-of-64 scene, 64 missed drones each; 90-109 ms for groups of 32). The PR's own split table shows it: the groups-of-64 row has an "other" mean of 22.2 ms across all 50 steps with 19 missed steps and about 5 ms on no-miss steps, which puts the missed steps near 50 ms on the author's machine, five times the reserve. The bound "part 1 + max(B - R - part 1, 25 ms) + R" therefore does not describe a missed step, which is the step the deadline exists for.

Required: measure part 3 on missed steps and size the reserve from that, or compute the pair constraints of a missed cluster once (as `_fallback_cluster` does) instead of once per drone. The 3.4 ms figure must be replaced or qualified in the doc.

Reproduce:

    python probe2d_bench_first.py      # "evasive" column: 120-145 ms per missed step at groups of 64

## Non-blocking findings

- **N1. Escape stage has no reserved time, and its misses are invisible.** Both stages share one absolute deadline (`apply_filter` passes the same `deadline` at lines 2034 and 2065). The doc's "both stages get W" reads as two windows; it is one. An escape problem that misses keeps the first solve and is flagged only through `escape_failed_slack_pairs`, the same flag as a genuine escape failure, and is not counted in `deadline_fallbacks`. A drone in a late escape problem with no slack pair of its own carries no flag at all. Give escape misses their own diagnostic (or count them in `deadline_fallbacks`) and fix the sentence.
- **N2. Training and policy evaluation run with the deadline on.** `training/rollout.py:63` and `training/evaluate.py` use `FilterConfig()`. Shielded rollouts, and the `utm-eval-policy` tables, now depend on machine load on steps with a miss. The manifest records `step_deadline: true`, so it is traceable, but `docs/training/QUICKSTART.md` says "the same seed gives the same weights". Pass `step_deadline=False` in evaluation (it is offline), and decide for training.
- **N3. Doc recount.** The fast suite here: 223 passed, 3 skipped, 2 xfailed (doc: 222 passed). Everything else recounted matches: 10000 jsonl rows, 5000 fuzz / 5000 hard, 0 fallback / solver-error / cap-hit / abort rows, 13 hard rows with `first_inside`, min `min_sep` 5.5336 m (fuzz) and 3.5682 m (hard), max rollout 66.
- **N4. Solved accelerations are clipped to +/- u_max, not to the drone's world-box limits** (`_solve_one_qp`). A KKT-tolerance excursion of up to about 4.5e-7 m/s^2 past a world-box limit can violate a dropped cross-cluster row by about 1e-6 m/s^2. Pre-existing, far below the intervention tolerance; noted for completeness because the cross-cluster proof in attack 1 rests on the box.
- **N5. `known limit` of `_evasive_accel` applies to every deadline miss.** Its docstring says it can push a drone towards a third neighbour. With the deadline, this is the controller of 64 drones on 19 of 50 steps in the PR's own 64-cluster run and on the first step of the target-density scene (B1). The doc's "safety cost" paragraph says this for clusters of 32+; it should say it for the first step at the target too, once B1 is measured.
- **N6. The floor regime is the normal regime at N = 64 on a slower machine.** Here every step of the 64-cluster and 32-cluster scenes solved in the 25 ms floor, i.e. the budget was already spent before the solves. The deadline then does not bound the step at 100 ms; it bounds the solves at 25 ms. The doc says this for N >= 128; it should say "when part 1 exceeds 65 ms", which depends on the machine.

## Verified correct

- **Attack 1, cross-cluster independence (probe1_cross.py).** 300 random scenes with 2-4 nearby groups of 2-5 drones, 277 with at least two conflict clusters, one cluster forced to miss each time (patched `_solve_one_qp`, 5 s budget so nothing else misses). All 12551 cross-cluster first-stage rows hold with the mixed accelerations (worst margin 3.22 m/s^2 of slack to spare, no violation). Every kept cluster's accelerations are bit-identical to the deadline-off run. Every evasive acceleration is inside its world-box limits. Reasoning this confirms: `_independent_problems` drops a row only when it holds over the whole acceleration box, and every output (QP solution within KKT tolerance, evasive clipped to the box, clipped nominal) stays in the box; the escape stage skips problems containing a missed agent and a (missed, kept) pair cannot have a binding backup row, so kept agents' escape problems exclude missed agents.
- **Attack 3, wall-clock independence without a miss (probe3_exact.py).** `head-on-40`, shielded reference stand-in, 297 steps: records (all actions, flags, diagnostics) bit-identical between deadline off, deadline on with a 10 s budget and deadline on with the standard budget; 0 deadline fallbacks. `_DEADLINE` is never stored in `reuse`; latches are updated before the solves and do not read the clock. `daqp.solve` accepts `time_limit` and rejects an unknown keyword with `TypeError`, so the limit is applied, not silently dropped.
- **Attack 4, the switch.** `step_deadline` defaults to `True`; the only places that turn it off are `tests/conftest.py` (pytest only, by rewriting the dataclass default) and every `scripts/verify/*.py` plus `scripts/disturbance_matrix.py`; `_warm_up` uses `step_deadline=False` and runs before `step_start` is taken (`filter.py:1923-1924`). Runtime callers (`runner.py`, `px4_bridge.py`, `px4_demo.py`, `rollout.py`, `evaluate.py`, `scale_bench.py`) construct `FilterConfig()` and get it on. No path runs the runtime with it off by accident; N2 is the reverse concern.
- **Attack 5, range gate and certificate.** Gate by hand: backup term at k = 108 (`_backup_max_steps` = 109, `traj[k]` is the state after k + 1 steps, consistent with the (k + 1) factor) is 5.5 + 2 * 15 * 109 * 0.1 + 2 * 12.124 * 108.5 * 0.1 = 595.6 m; escape sqrt(3) * (5.5 + 90 + 54.56) = 259.9 m with m . n >= 1/sqrt(3) from the escape frame; braking 167 m. `DroneState` rejects speeds above 15 m/s, so the v_max assumption holds for every input. Certificate (probe5_cert.py), on a 3-ring, a collinear squeeze with degenerate duals (first-stage and escape rows) and a 16-ring escape QP: every OSQP answer off the DAQP optimum by more than 1e-6 m/s^2 is rejected (eps 1e-3 to 1e-9 without polish, eps 1e-3 and 1e-4 with polish on the hard cases), every polished answer within 1e-8 is accepted, perturbations of the exact optimum of 1e-7 and above are rejected on three of four QPs and 1e-6 and above on all. The 16-ring escape QP at eps 1e-4 with polish is 0.34 m/s^2 off and rejected.
- **Attack 6, claims vs jsonl.** See N3.
- `SafeAction.deadline_fallback` requires `fallback_used` (validator); `qp_feasible` stays true on a miss as documented; `runner.py` counts `deadline_fallbacks` per agent-step as the doc describes.
