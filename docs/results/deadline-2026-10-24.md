# Bounded step time: per-cluster solve deadlines, 2026-10-24

Founder decision 2026-10-24 (before merging PR #26):
- each conflict cluster gets a solve deadline derived from the 100 ms step budget;
- clusters are solved most urgent first;
- a cluster not certified by its deadline flies its backup action for the step;
- one cluster never delays another.

Branch perf/solver-exact at 51d3527. Machine as in `scale-2026-10-24.md` (4-core Xeon KVM, Linux, Python 3.12.3). Reference: `solver-exact-2026-10-24.jsonl`.

## Derivation of the deadline

The filter decides once per step, B = 100 ms (`SIMULATION_STEP_MS`). A step has three parts:

1. **Work before the solves:** pair loop, latches, backup rollout. Its cost is fixed by N; there is no iteration in it.
2. **The solves:** first stage and escape stage, one QP per cluster. Only this part iterates, and it is what was unbounded (a single 64-drone cluster took up to 55 s).
3. **Work after the solves:** escape-pair selection, backup actions for missed clusters, diagnostics, SafeAction construction. Measured at most 3.4 ms at N = 64 and 9.6 ms at N = 128.

The deadline is built in three layers:

- **Reserve for part 3:** R = 10 ms (`_POST_SOLVE_RESERVE_S`), covering the measured 9.6 ms.
- **Solve window:** both stages get W = (step start + B - R) - (time the solves start). When part 1 already used the budget (N ≥ 128 on this machine), they still get `_MIN_SOLVE_WINDOW_S` = 25 ms, above the measured 99.9th percentile of a whole solve stage at N = 64 (16.5 ms; 2.5 ms in fuzz). So a step takes at most (part 1) + max(B - R - part 1, 25 ms) + R, plus the overrun of one solver call past its time limit:
  - DAQP checks its time limit inside its iterations;
  - OSQP's limit excludes its setup, about 10 ms at 2208 variables.
- **Per cluster** (`_cluster_deadlines`): the clusters with rows that can bind are sorted by urgency, the smallest backup margin d* - d_eff of their rows first. Cluster k has a guaranteed share r_k = W · n_k / Σ n of the window (n = QP variables). Its deadline is the window end minus the shares of the clusters after it. An urgent cluster can therefore use time the others leave, but never another cluster's share: if cluster k runs to its deadline, cluster k + 1 still starts with at least r_(k+1). DAQP and OSQP get the time left until the cluster's deadline as their own time limit.

**On a miss:**
- A cluster not certified by its deadline flies its backup manoeuvre for the step: each drone takes `_evasive_accel` against its most critical neighbour in the cluster, the evasive manoeuvre the backup rows simulate, clipped to the world-box limits. This follows the backup-CBF fallback: a backup controller that only has to keep the system safe on its own, used when the filter's QP is not available (Gurriet, Mote, Singletary, Nilsson, Feron and Ames, "A scalable safety critical control framework for nonlinear systems", *IEEE Access* 8, 2020; Chen, Jankovic, Santillo and Ames, "Backup control barrier functions: formulation and comparative study", IEEE CDC 2021). Here the backup is the pairwise evasive manoeuvre, so the guarantee those papers give for a verified backup does not carry over (BL-02, BL-03). Its drones are flagged `deadline_fallback` and `fallback_used` (contract v1.5) and counted in `deadline_fallbacks`.
- A cluster whose escape re-solve misses its deadline keeps its first solve, as when the escape re-solve fails.
- The escape stage reuses the first-stage solution of every problem with no escape row. This is bit-identical: the same QP to deterministic solvers.

What the tables show:
- **At the target nothing changes.** N = 64 dense (groups of 8) p95 62 ms and city 62 ms, with no deadline hit. Groups of 2, 4 and 16 also have no hit.
- **Worst-case step time is bounded.** The single 64-drone cluster's worst step is 134 ms (152 ms on one thread), against 55 s without the deadline. Its p95 is 128 ms against 6405 ms. Above 100 ms is part 1 (latches and rollout for a 64-drone cluster) plus the 25 ms floor plus the reserve.
- **N = 128** is bounded by part 1 (175-300 ms), which no deadline can shorten. Its solve stage now gets the 25 ms floor, so some steps miss: 13 of 50 dense, 5 of 50 city. Min separation stays above d_eff (5.612 m dense, 8.019 m city).

**The safety cost, for the founder.** When a cluster's QP cannot be certified in the window, its drones fly the pairwise backup manoeuvre. That backup is the unverified heuristic of BL-02 and BL-03: a drone with several neighbours evades only its most critical one. In clusters of 32 or 64 drones this happens on many steps.

- a consistent per-drone backup (BL-03), so a missed cluster flies a backup that accounts for all neighbours;
- a strategic layer that keeps clusters small (the founder's scope note).

## (3) Tests

`tests/test_filter_deadline.py` uses two separate three-drone encounters in one sky, so there are two clusters:

- `test_two_clusters_are_solved_separately_most_urgent_first`: the closer encounter (smaller backup margin) is solved first, and no deadline is hit without forcing.
- `test_forced_deadline_on_one_cluster_backs_it_up_and_leaves_the_other`: cluster a's solve returns "deadline". Its drones fly `_evasive_accel` with `deadline_fallback`, `fallback_used` and not `qp_feasible`. The step's `qp_feasible` stays true. Cluster b's accelerations are bit-identical to the run without the forced miss.
- `test_a_slow_cluster_does_not_delay_the_other`: cluster a (solved first) gets a solver that spins until its own deadline and gives up, as DAQP and OSQP do at their time limit. Cluster b still gets its share and its normal solution, bit for bit, and the step ends within the budget plus margin.
- `test_cluster_deadlines_guarantee_each_share`: starting each cluster at the previous cluster's deadline (the worst case) still leaves it its share.
- `test_episode_summary_counts_deadline_fallbacks`: the episode summary counts `deadline_fallbacks`, and `fallback_events` includes them.

## Known limits

- The deadline is wall-clock time, so a result can depend on machine load, but only on steps with a deadline miss, and those are flagged and counted. Off those steps the filter is deterministic (the full tier above, and `det.py` identical across processes).
- The bound covers the solves. The work before them (latches, rollout) grows with N and is not cut by any deadline. It decides whether a step fits in 100 ms at N ≥ 128, or on one thread at N = 64 (p95 106 ms dense).
- Part 1 at a 64-drone cluster (latches with many entering pairs) pushes that case's step past 100 ms even with the solves bounded.
