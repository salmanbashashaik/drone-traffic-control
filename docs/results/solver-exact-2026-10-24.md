# KKT-exact filter QP and the new per-seed reference, 2026-10-24

Founder decision 2026-10-24: option 1 of `docs/architecture/PROPOSAL-scale-qp.md`. Branch perf/solver-exact, rebased on main 75b41c0. **Status: candidate reference, for the founder's review.** Per-cluster QPs and the range gate (step 3) wait until this reference is accepted.

Machine: Intel Xeon @ 2.80 GHz (KVM), 4 logical cores, Linux 6.18, Python 3.12.3, numpy 1.26.4, scipy 1.17.1, osqp 0.6.7.post3, daqp 0.10.3, numba 0.68.0.

## What changed

`_solve_hocbf_qp` builds the same QP as before (`build_hocbf_qp_matrices`, unchanged). It then solves it with `_solve_exact`:

- **QPs up to `_EXACT_MAX_VARS` = 256 variables:** DAQP first, OSQP second. DAQP is a dual active-set solver (Arnström, Bemporad and Axehill, *IEEE TAC* 67(8), 2022), and its outer proximal-point iterations handle our singular Hessian (linearly penalised slacks).
- **Larger QPs:** OSQP first (eps 1e-9 instead of 1e-5, `max_iter` 200 000), DAQP second.
- **Certificate:** a solution is used only if it passes `_kkt_certified`. That checks primal feasibility per row (relative to the bound), stationarity per component against |Px| + |q| + |A|ᵀ|y| (OSQP's own form), multiplier signs and complementarity. The tolerances were set from measured residuals.
- **No certified solution** from either solver is a solver error, which leads to the existing fallback.

The optimum is unique: the objective is strictly convex in the accelerations, and each slack is then fixed. A certified solution is therefore that optimum, whichever solver found it and whichever drones share the QP. Step 3 relies on this.

Other changes:
- `daqp>=0.10.3,<1` added to `pyproject.toml`. Licence MIT; wheels exist for Windows (cp312 win_amd64), Linux (cp311 manylinux) and macOS (cp312 arm64), checked with `pip download`.
- The tests and `targeted.py` that simulate a solver crash now break DAQP as well as OSQP. Their expectations are unchanged.

## Why DAQP plus tight OSQP (measured)

Measured with `scripts/verify/solver_compare.py --fuzz 150 --hard 150 --bench 16 32 64`; the full output is at the end. It records every QP the filter builds on fuzz seeds 0-149 (both modes) and the joint QPs of `scale_bench` at N = 16, 32, 64 (dense, groups of 8). Each QP is then solved by every solver.

- **Old OSQP (eps 1e-5, polish) was not exact.** On the fuzz QPs it was off by more than 1e-6 m/s² on 158 of 21 259 QPs (0.7 %), by up to 0.05 m/s², and 158 failed the certificate. The polish step guesses the active set from the ADMM iterate and accepts a guess that only lowers residuals (Stellato et al., OSQP, *Math. Prog. Comp.* 12(4), 2020). In one case it snapped a drone onto a box bound it was 0.015 m/s² inside.
- **The three accurate solvers agree:** DAQP, OSQP at eps 1e-9 and proxsuite agree within 5e-8 m/s² on every one of the 21 259 fuzz QPs, and all three pass the certificate on all of them. Interior-point solvers are less accurate: Clarabel agrees to 4e-7 (9 QPs uncertified) and PIQP to 1.3e-5 (11 QPs above 1e-6, 143 uncertified).
- **Speed:**
  - On fuzz-size QPs (7-33 variables), DAQP costs the same as the old OSQP (0.56 against 0.62 ms mean) and 15x less than OSQP at eps 1e-9 (8 ms).
  - On a joint N = 16 QP (168 variables), DAQP is still faster than tight OSQP (2.3 against 12 ms).
  - At N = 32 (592 variables), tight OSQP is faster (49 against 117 ms).
  - At N = 64 (2208 variables), DAQP's dense algebra takes about 18 s per QP, tight OSQP 333 ms.
  - Hence the 256-variable switch. Step 3's per-cluster QPs (52 variables for a group of 8) are well inside DAQP's range.
- **Proxsuite** is as fast as DAQP on fuzz-size QPs (0.63 ms) but 13x slower at 168 variables (30 ms) and slower than tight OSQP on joint QPs (74 s at N = 64). DAQP is preferred for small QPs: it is faster or equal at every small size, and as an active-set method it ends at an exact vertex of the active set. **Clarabel and PIQP** are fast on large QPs (80 and 45 ms at N = 64) but fail the certificate on some QPs and are only 1e-7 to 1e-5 accurate.

## Known limits

- The reference was generated on this Linux machine. On another platform (the founder's Windows laptop, CI on Python 3.11) DAQP and OSQP round differently. With an exact solver the per-seed differences should be at round-off level, not the 1e-2 m/s² of the old OSQP, but this was not measured on a second platform.
- The certificate tolerances (`_KKT_TOL` 1e-7, `_KKT_STATIONARITY_RTOL` 1e-10) are set from residuals measured on the filter's QPs, not derived. OSQP's escape-QP solutions often fail them, so those QPs go to DAQP.
- `compare_results.py` and the quick tier still use `pr12-b1-2026-10-23.jsonl` until the founder accepts the new reference. The quick tier's extra hard seeds (the reference's runs inside d_eff) are the same 13 in both files.
