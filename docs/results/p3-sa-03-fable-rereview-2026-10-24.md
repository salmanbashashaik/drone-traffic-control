## Task 3: the lost-command term

### Is the bound correct?

The term is `lost_m` = 0.1 · dc_max · (AGE_S + T) per drone, with dc_max = 2√2 a_phys (`margin.py:83-96`), AGE_S = 0.46 s, T from `stopping_s`. The model: a lost 100 ms command leaves its predecessor in force for one segment (PX4 hold-last; `sim.py:384-399` and `415-419`), so the drone's acceleration differs from the logged one by at most dc_max for 0.1 s, a velocity error of at most 0.1 · dc_max. The server learns of the loss only through the echo, at most AGE_S later (`margin.py:54-60`; older telemetry raises `link`). If the backup must start now, the open-loop backup from the server's estimate inherits that velocity error for the whole horizon T. Position error at the end of the horizon is then at most 0.1 · dc_max · (AGE_S + T). The first-order lag delays the acceleration error but does not change its integral, so the bound holds through the lag. **The bound is correct as a worst case under the branch's model.** The reasoning in §3.6 is sound.

### Is it needlessly loose?

Yes, in three places, in decreasing order of size. None is a blocker; each is a design change that needs its own derivation and a new matrix.

1. **The horizon piece ignores the drag.** The plant and the rollout both damp a velocity error through the airspeed drag D (w − v): a velocity perturbation decays as e^(−Dt), so its position effect over T is δv · (1 − e^(−DT)) / D, not δv · T. With D = 0.2462 s⁻¹ and T = 6.445 s that is 3.23 s against 6.45 s, a factor of two. The rollout already has the exact lag-plus-drag step matrices (`rollout.step_matrices`, `rollout.py:50-55`) and a sensitivity array (`rollout.sensitivity`, `rollout.py:59-66`): the lost term can be the position response of those matrices to an acceleration impulse of area 0.1 · dc_max, which is how van Wijk et al. (2024) treat a bounded input disturbance in a backup CBF. A safe version uses the lower bound of D (the drag uncertainty K2 σ_u is already in `headroom`), so the gain is smaller than a factor of two but still large.
2. **dc_max is the airframe's authority, not the filter's command set.** The filter's output is bounded per axis by u_max = 3.5 m/s², so two successive filter commands differ by at most 2√3 · 3.5 = 12.12 m/s² plus the change of the drag trim added at `sim.py:392` (`u − preds.drag`, bounded by D times the change in estimated airspeed per cycle, a few tenths of m/s²). The term uses 16.0 m/s² (30° airframes) and 19.4 m/s² (35°). Using the filter's bound plus a trim bound is a 25–35 % reduction. It requires that the server never sends anything outside the filter's box, which holds in the simulation (`_authority` clips, but the filter's box is tighter).
3. **The error is observable and the term treats it as never measured.** A velocity error of 1.6–1.9 m/s against a velocity measurement noise of 0.074–0.102 m/s (`envelope.py`) is corrected by the next Kalman update, and `nis` would fire on it (B3 shows the NIS test catching a 5× underreport in 16 of 16 runs). The brief's question ("given the backup is re-evaluated from the measured state at the next cycle") has two parts. The re-evaluation does not remove the horizon piece for the certificate the barrier gives: if the loss happens on the cycle at which the backup must start, the executed backup carries the error for its whole duration, and the margin must cover that. What it removes is the assumption that the error survives the undetected window unmeasured: after at most one message (not AGE_S plus an echo) the tracker has seen it. A tighter but still deterministic term would bound the residual velocity error after one position-and-velocity update (one minus the Kalman gain times δv, plus the gain times the noise at K1), which needs a derivation of the worst-case gain under the tracker's floor (`tracker.floored`, `tracker.py:242-250`). I did not do that derivation; it is a design step, not a review step.

The AGE_S piece (0.46 of 6.9 s, 7 %) is the right quantity for the undetected window and is not loose in any meaningful way. Note also that `CommandLog.confirm` (`tracker.py:91-113`) corrects the log when the echo arrives but the tracker's posterior mean is not re-propagated from the pre-loss state; the correction reaches the estimate only through later measurement updates. That is why option (c) in §8 (re-prediction from a stored pre-loss posterior) would help, and it supports keeping the AGE_S piece deterministic until that is done.

### Is §8 option (b) sound, and what must it prove?

Option (b) sends each drone a short command plan per message (the next few commands), so a lost message leaves the drone executing the previous plan's next step instead of holding the last command; §8 cites Singletary et al. (2020) for the input buffer. I checked the literature myself.

- **Singletary, Chen, Ames (2020), "Control Barrier Functions for Sampled-Data Systems with Input Delays" (arXiv 2005.06418, CDC 2020).** Algorithm 1 keeps a buffer of the last n inputs and integrates the dynamics forward over the known delay n·Δt to the state at which the new input will act (their Eq. 18, x_{(i+n)Δt} = φ^{u_H}_{nΔt}(x_{iΔt})); the CBF constraint is evaluated at that predicted state. Assumption 1: the delay is a known integer multiple of the controller period. Packet loss or a missing input is **not** considered. Corollary 1 extends the result to a bounded integration error Δx (a robust invariant set). The paper gives the delay-compensation structure for option (b) but says nothing about loss; the LTE model here (log-normal delay, `P_TICKS` = 13 bound, late messages dropped at `sim.py:399`) makes a late message a lost one, so the loss case is the whole point.
- **Quevedo and Nešić (2011), "Input-to-state stability of packetized predictive control over unreliable networks affected by packet-dropouts", IEEE TAC 56(2):370–375**, and their 2012 Automatica paper for Markovian losses. Packetized predictive control (PPC) is exactly option (b): the controller sends a sequence of inputs, the actuator buffers it and plays it on loss. The 2011 result is input-to-state stability provided the number of **consecutive** dropouts is bounded and the plan is at least that long. The result is about stability, not a barrier; the proof technique (the plan's tail is a feasible continuation of the previous plan) is the one a safety version needs.
- **Seidel, Anand, Allgöwer (2026), "Safety for Weakly-Hard Control Systems via Graph-Based Barrier Functions" (arXiv 2601.00494).** Barrier functions for systems where dropouts are bounded within a window (weakly-hard (m, k) constraints) with a hold or zero actuator strategy. This is the closest published safety result to option (b) and to the current hold-last model; it is recent and I did not read beyond the abstract.
- **Backup set invariance.** Gurriet et al. (2018, ICCPS) and Chen, Singletary, Ames (2021, IEEE L-CSS 5(1):127–132, "Guaranteed Obstacle Avoidance for Multi-Robot Operations with Limited Actuation") give the recursive-feasibility argument that executing the first step of a certified backup keeps the next backup feasible. Option (b) is that argument applied with the drone executing the backup prefix on loss.

So option (b) is sound in principle, and the literature supports its two halves (delay buffer, loss playback) separately. What it must prove, in order:

1. **There is a single per-drone plan to send.** The backup-set barrier certifies one backup per pair (`_backup_constraints` is pairwise; `rollout.pair_rollout` too), and the backups a drone is certified against can differ between its pairs. A drone can only play one plan. Option (b) therefore presupposes the consistent per-drone backup (BL-03, the CBK result on `research/consistent-backup`: "adapt, do not adopt"). Without it there is no certified plan tail to send, and sending the filter's own future output is not certified at all. This is the structural precondition and the note does not state it.
2. **Recursive feasibility under "play the previous plan's step on loss".** The invariance argument of Gurriet / Chen must be redone with sampled data (Singletary 2020 Theorem 1 handles the inter-sample reachable set), the first-order actuator lag (the plan lives in thrust-command space and the rollout already integrates the lag), the margin's estimate error Δx (Corollary 1 structure), and the drag and wind uncertainty of this branch.
3. **A bound on consecutive losses, and a model that supports it.** With the iid 3GPP 10⁻³ loss model, k consecutive losses have probability 10⁻³ᵏ per cycle start; against the per-check risk q = target / 36 000 = 2.8 · 10⁻¹⁰ at 10⁻⁵ per pair-hour, k = 3 (10⁻⁹) is not enough and k = 4 (10⁻¹²) is, so a plan of at least four steps beyond the current command. Real LTE losses are bursty, so the iid model understates the burst tail; the honest model is the weakly-hard (m, k) one of Seidel et al., with m and k measured on the actual link, which does not exist yet.
4. **The residual ambiguity term.** Until the echo arrives, the server does not know whether the drone played the new command c_t or the previous plan's step b_{t−1}. The lost term does not vanish; it becomes 0.1 · |c_t − b_{t−1}| through the lag-and-drag response over the undetected window and horizon, instead of 0.1 · dc_max. When the barrier is active the filter's output is close to the backup, so the term is small exactly when it matters; a bound on |c_t − b_{t−1}| in the worst case must still be derived and will not be zero when the nominal policy and the backup disagree.
5. **The autopilot can play a timed plan.** PX4 offboard mode has no buffered acceleration schedule; a companion computer or the onboard safety layer (not built; CLAUDE.md "likely direction") must execute plan step k at absolute tick t + k. `CommandLog.plan(t + P_TICKS, c)` (`sim.py:395`) already schedules by absolute tick, which is the right interface. This makes option (b) an onboard-layer feature and an architecture decision (ADR before any build).

Option (a) in §8 (shorten the open-loop horizon) and option (c) (re-predict from a stored pre-loss posterior) are smaller and do not need BL-03; given item 1, the order should be (c), then the drag-aware response of looseness item 1 above, then (b) only after CBK is adopted in some form.

## Task 4: can the stale `rollout.CTX` defect change a headline number?

Mechanism (confirmed by probe). `run.run` (`run.py:79-80`) computes `sim.recoverable(p0, v0)` for a new scenario before `sim.episode` runs, so `rollout.CTX` still holds the previous adaptive episode's entries `d0..d(n−1)` (set at `sim.py:353`). `recoverable` calls `harness.kinematic("main", ...)`, whose filter reaches the rebound `F._backup_constraints` (`rollout.py:184`). Two cases:

- previous episode had **fewer** drones: `pair_rollout` indexes `CTX[aid]` for an id that is not there (`rollout.py:148`), raises `KeyError`, `apply_filter` catches it at `filter.py:1443` and applies the evasive fallback. Probe: a `headon2` adaptive episode followed by `recoverable` on `ring4` seed 0 gives `min_sep` 9.986 m with 220 solver errors and 18 fallback cycles; with `CTX` cleared, 6.160 m with none. The recorded `ideal_min_sep` for `ring4` seed 0 is 9.986 in all six nominal conditions, so this is what happened in the matrix.
- previous episode had **at least as many** drones: the lagged rollout runs with the other scenario's actuator states, drag and authority. Probe: `ring4` seed 1 then `ring4` seed 0 gives 6.069 m against 6.160 m.

Can it change a headline number? `ideal_min_sep` enters the tables only through `_in_limit` (`run.py:105-106`, the 5 m class) and the "Excluded" count; the episodes themselves never read it. So the only path is a class flip. I recomputed every scenario's recoverable value with `CTX` cleared (144 unique cleared-speed scenarios, 1184 rows, about 40 s on four processes; script `cleanrec.py`): 895 of 1184 recorded values differ from the clean ones, the largest by 4.351 m (`random` seed 7, calm: 10.82 recorded, 6.469 clean), and **zero scenarios change class**. The in-limit sets and all counts stand: 218 / 203 adaptive rows with 0 LoS and 89 / 88 baseline LoS at 10⁻⁵ / 10⁻⁷ with either column. The DECISIONS statement ("changed no scenario's class; only random seed 4 is unrecoverable") is confirmed. The closest in-limit scenarios sit at 5.51–5.54 m clean, so the margin to a flip was about 0.5 m while the defect's shifts reached 4.35 m; the result holds for this dataset, not by construction, which is why R1 asks for the column to be recomputed and the fix committed before the next matrix.

## Task 5: anything new that blocks moving the tracker, command log, delay prediction and monitor into main as diagnostics?

Items D1–D3 above. Two further observations that are not blockers:

- `Tracker.update` (`tracker.py:214-233`) drops any message whose tick is not later than the posterior's tick. On a real downlink with reordering, dropped messages reduce the measurement rate silently; a diagnostic on main should count them.
- `LagMonitor`'s windows overlap, so its false-alarm rate per run is above `P_RISK` per test (recorded known limit). As a diagnostic on main its alarm must be labelled uncalibrated; the data show 1 false alarm in 48 nominal SBAS calm runs and 2–3 in 48 per takeoff grade.

Dependencies are not a problem: `tracker.py` needs only NumPy, SciPy (`expm`, `chi2`, `f`), `shared.contracts.MAX_SPEED_MPS` and two constants from `envelope.py`; SciPy and numba are already main dependencies (`pyproject.toml` lines 11–19 on `main`).

## Non-blocking observations

- N-a. The note and `run.table` use different median conventions for even-sized groups (Task 2); pick one.
- N-b. "Raised no warning at all" (note line 447) means "no alarm other than `absorb` or `margin`"; 45 / 38 runs had no alarm of any kind. Say which.
- N-c. `sim.recoverable` (`sim.py:182`) mutates `harness.MAX_SPEED_MPS`, a module global of another research package; harmless here, but another reason not to move `sim.py`.
- N-d. The B1 term evaluates S at the horizon end for every rollout row (`margin.py:179`); this is conservative and simple, and could be tightened row by row with the same sensitivity machinery if the lost term is reworked.
