## Findings

1. **B6 has no silent LoS.** There is no loss of separation under either grade, so the escalation condition for group 1 is not met. The lag alarm came at 9.8–11.9 s under RTK (median 11.5 s) and at 7.4–12.0 s under SBAS (median 10.5 s). That is often less than 1 s before the encounter, so the margin, not the alarm, kept these runs apart. Under SBAS the margin is 23 m, and the shortest distance was 17.6 m against 13.0 m in the first run (old plant). Under RTK the margin is 1.4–2.5 m and the shortest distance 7.9 m.
2. **Lag detection gap under RTK.** `headon2` seeds 2 and 3 had no lag alarm; they kept 9.08 m and 10.59 m. A head-on pair commands almost constant thrust until the encounter, and the lag monitor needs command changes to tell lags apart (`check_lag_id`, the flat case). This follows from the design and caused no loss, but the monitor cannot be counted on to find a slow actuator before the first manoeuvre.
3. **`baseline_lag` and `baseline` give the same result.** 140 of 144 run pairs are identical, and the other 4 differ by at most 0.15 m. Main's default barrier is `backup` (contract v1.3). Its docstring states "lag_s only lengthens the horizon", and the backup rollout already flies until the pair stops closing. The `lag_s * closing_speed` reserve applies only to the `braking` barrier. Decision 7's baseline therefore budgets for almost no lag. This is main's documented behaviour and not a change in this lane.
4. **Main's filter does worse on this plant than in the first run.** Recoverable LoS rose from 7 to 17 of 47 under RTK, from 37 to 39 under SBAS and from 37 to 43 under GPS SPS. The first run flew the old plant, and decisions 1 and 2a changed it. SBAS and SPS now use the guaranteed 95 % figures (7.6 m, 8 / 13 m), not the measured ones. The drag plant opposes lateral evasion. The baseline's trim `D v_route` also pushes along the route while the filter brakes, so braking is weaker than main's double-integrator model assumes. That the drag and the trim explain the RTK change is a hypothesis. It has not been checked yet, because it needs a diagnostic run, which waits for the matrix to finish.
5. **B8 under SBAS: one silent LoS.** In `ring4` seed 5, separation was lost at 11.76 s and the minimum was 3.10 m. The only alarm was `headroom` at 2.0 s, at warm-up. The first version of this report did not count the run as silent; under the founder's decision it is a silent LoS in a beyond-limit case (the receiver under-reports its error five times). A position bias that matches the reported σ leaves the innovations consistent, and no monitor in the design tests for it. In `ring4` seed 0, separation was lost at 12.89 s (minimum 2.36 m), and `absorb` had fired at 8.4 s, 4.5 s before. Neither run is worse than main's baseline, which loses separation in 39 of 47 SBAS calm runs, and B8 had no earlier run to compare with. The claim that a beyond-limit fault raises an alarm before the LoS does not hold for B8 under SBAS.

## Founder decisions on this report (2026-10-24)

1. **"Alarm before LoS" leaves out the warm-up `headroom` alarm.** B8 SBAS `ring4/5` is recorded as a silent loss of separation in a beyond-limit case. The matrix was not stopped. `run._first_alarm` implements the rule; `check.check_silent_los` checks it.
2. **No bespoke bias detector.** The design note gains a literature section on GNSS integrity (P3-SA-03 §2.5) and a design change, documented only: the margin's GNSS term uses the receiver's protection level in place of the reported σ where the receiver provides a qualifying one, and dense corridors require integrity-capable positioning (§3.10). B8 is stated as a limit of single-receiver GNSS without integrity.
3. **No `braking`-barrier baseline.** After the matrix, a diagnostic run checks why main's RTK baseline went from 7 to 17 losses (the drag plant against the trim along the route). Its result is in P3-SA-03 §6.
4. **Lag from B6.** The margin uses the drone's declared upper-bound lag until the lag identifier has enough excitation, and the identifier reports "not identifiable" instead of staying silent. This is documented in P3-SA-03 §3.10 and not prototyped: no source gives an upper-bound lag for the fleet, and changing the margin partway through the matrix would mix two designs in one set of rows (`docs/DECISIONS.md`).
